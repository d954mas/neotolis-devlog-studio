# Gamedev Reels research — initial sample

Captured 2026-07-19. The sample contains 10 public Instagram authors and 113
recent Reels. An outlier score compares a Reel's views with the median views of
the collected Reels from the same author. This prevents a large account from
winning only because it already has a large audience.

## Early findings

1. **The viewer premise beats the production update.**
   [“Would you play for 400 real days?”](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dauke9vNBdP/)
   reached 652,516 views and 32.11x the author's baseline. It leads with a
   concrete player fantasy and a number, not “I added a feature.”
2. **A familiar pain plus an immediate promise travels outside the niche.**
   [“Yeah, I failed the donut too…”](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DaWfk4aSk2A/)
   reached 525,605 views and 25.77x baseline. This is not a model to copy
   literally; it is evidence for recognition-first framing.
3. **Emotion outperforms generic advice.**
   [A montage of emotional story-game moments](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYC0SLTPr2w/)
   reached 1,579,671 views and 17.73x baseline. The subject is the feeling a
   player wants, not the creator's workflow.
4. **A compact game pitch works when it contains a sharp differentiator.**
   [The Attractor K7 pitch](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DagBDyRogH2/)
   combines local identity, a known genre, a world-changing anomaly, Russian
   voice-over, demo timing, and a wishlist CTA; it reached 11.18x baseline.
5. **Honest failure can make a devlog legible.**
   [Helyana's rework update](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYq79eSisMV/)
   names poor execution and lost performance before showing recovery; it
   reached 454,423 views and 9.09x baseline.

## What not to conclude yet

- Caption text is not the same as the first spoken line or first frame.
- Twelve recent Reels per author is enough to find candidates, not to prove a
  universal rule.
- Tutorial and game-curation accounts are format references, not identities to
  imitate. The user's own game, voice, footage, humour, and claims remain the
  content source.
- Views alone do not prove wishlists or sales. Experiments must measure the
  user's own reach, engagement, profile actions, and wishlist movement.

## Three safe first experiments

1. **High-concept question:** open on real gameplay with one concrete player
   fantasy and a number or constraint. Keep the user's own tone and footage.
2. **Failure to visible fix:** show the broken mechanic in frame one, explain
   one causal change, then show the improved result.
3. **One-mechanic pitch:** familiar genre + one differentiator + player stakes +
   one honest CTA. Avoid a list of features.

Before producing any of these, manually review the top two Reels per author and
annotate first frame, first spoken sentence, cut rhythm, captions, proof shot,
and CTA. Create an experiment only after choosing which observation to test.
